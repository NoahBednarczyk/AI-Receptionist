# Outbound calling: what I found (2026-11-01). Research, not legal advice.

I am not a lawyer and this is not a clearance. It is what public sources say, so the bot stays in the safe modes until a person confirms.

## Findings
1. **AI voice counts as an "artificial voice" under the TCPA** (FCC declaratory ruling, Feb 2024). Not a ban, but the usual consent rules apply, and marketing calls to cell or residential lines need prior express *written* consent. Sources: [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2024/02/Did-the-FCC-Really-Ban-AI-Generated-Voice-Calls-No-But-Restrictions-Apply), [Hunton](https://huntonak.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/fcc-issues-declaratory-ruling-that-tcpa-applies-to-ai-generated-voice-calls). Many small-shop numbers are cell numbers, so cold calling those is the main exposure.
2. **No federal rule yet requires saying you are an AI.** The FCC proposed one in 2024 (AI disclosure in the call plus separate consent); as of mid-2026 sources say it is not final ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ai-generated-calls-require-disclosure-new-fcc-proposal-requires-disclosure-ai-usage)). Some states (California, Utah, Colorado) have bot-disclosure laws with their own conditions. I did not find an Idaho one. Not saying "AI" up front is therefore not clearly illegal today for an Idaho call; denying it when asked, or claiming to be human, is the line, and the bot never does either.
3. **The FTC's 2024 Telemarketing Sales Rule update extends the ban on misrepresentation to business-to-business calls** ([Hunton](https://hunton.com/insights/legal/telemarketing-sales-rule-changes-remove-exception-for-business-to-business-calls-and-impose-new-recordkeeping-requirements), [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-04-16/pdf/2024-07180.pdf)). So the bot may persuade but may not mislead about what it sells, results, or who is calling. That is why the playbook says "persuade, never mislead", and why the opener names the caller, the company and that it is a sales call.
4. **Idaho Telephone Solicitation Act (Idaho Code 48-1001 and following)**: a secondary source says solicitors must register with the Attorney General and renew yearly; the statute has a no-solicitation-contact list for residential/mobile numbers ([statute](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title48/T48CH10/SECT48-1001), [summary](https://www.alfainternational.com/compendium/business-litigation-2/telephone-consumer-protection-act-tcpa/idaho)). Not verified: whether Ringback must register, the fee, and the business-call exemptions. **Ask the Idaho Attorney General's consumer protection office before the first live call.**
5. **Do-not-call requests must be honored**, which is why a plain "stop calling / take me off your list" ends the call in code.

## What this means for the switches
- `dry-run` (default): places nothing.
- `warm-only`: only shops marked `consent: yes`. For marketing calls with an AI voice to cell numbers, "consent" should mean written consent (a signed form, a text or email reply saying yes to AI-voiced calls), not just "they were friendly once".
- `cold`: stays off. Turning it on needs: written confirmation from a lawyer or the Idaho AG office that the calls (to landlines only) are allowed and whether to register; a landline check on every number; and a do-not-call list in use.
- Unverified: a June 2026 FCC ruling mentioned by one developer blog. Check fcc.gov.
